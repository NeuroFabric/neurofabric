*** THE FORECAST CANNOT BE MISTAKEN FOR A MEASUREMENT ***

Three ways a projection lies on a chart, and all three are now asserted:

- DASHED. A solid line continuing past the last observation is indistinguishable
  from data at a glance and in a screenshot. The reader has no way to check which
  is which, so it is dashed.
- BANDS DRAWN, not just the line. A dashed line without a band is a point
  estimate that happens to be dashed; the translucent range is the honest part,
  and it is painted UNDER the projection so it does not grey the line out.
- IT CAN BE TURNED OFF. The control most likely to be deleted in a tidy-up,
  because a toggle is one more thing. A reader who wants the past must not be
  forced to look at a claim about the future.

The projection is appended to the SAME axis rather than drawn as a second panel,
which is what makes the band read as a widening range; a separate panel would
let the two scales disagree.

The first projected point repeats the last observed value so the line connects.
Without it the two series float apart and read as unrelated facts.

Tooltip text on a projected point says PROJECTED, gives the range, and says it
came from the observed trend rather than a measurement.

*** A REFUSAL IS RENDERED, NOT HIDDEN ***

The control is replaced by that sentence rather than becoming a toggle that
