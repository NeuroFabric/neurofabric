feat(ui): the agent list, the quiet feed explained, and two real surfaces

Slices 4, 5 and 6 (F6c, F6a, F6b). The frontend suite was red on a half-finished
rename when this started; it is green now, and the three slices are committed as
one because they share the same stylesheet and the same surface story.

SLICE 4 -- THE AGENT LIST REPLACES THE <select>.

This is the primary navigation of the product and it rendered as a form control.
AgentList is a <nav> of real <button>s, so focus, Tab and Enter come from the
browser rather than from a file re-implementing them. The selected row is marked
by aria-current AND a filled shape, not by colour alone, because a reader who
cannot distinguish the accent is not left with nothing. A "No agent open" row is
a real button rather than a heading, so a null selection stays reachable the same
way every other row is.

NO STATUS DOT, DELIBERATELY, and a test says so. F6c's stated prize is a per-agent
status marker, and AgentDescriptor carries no per-agent status -- it is a title, a
domain, a version and a maturity, while the live verdict is one provider-wide
answer. A dot drawn now could not mean anything, and an indicator that cannot
mean anything teaches an operator to glance and be right by accident. What the
slice delivers instead is the LIST, so a dot has somewhere honest to live later.

The cost is stated in the file rather than discovered: arrow-key roving focus is
gone, which a <select> had for free.

SLICE 5 -- THE COUNT AND THE RATE, EXPLAINED.

The runbook's strongest beat is 0 events/sec beside a record count holding at
20,000, and an operator who concludes one of them is broken stops reading the
panel. Both numbers were always correct; the ambiguity is what is removed. When
the rate is zero and records are held, the readout now says nothing new arrived
in the interval, says the records are still held from earlier and nothing here
clears them, and says that this is a quiet feed rather than a stopped one -- and
points at the last-answer age as where a fault would actually show.

THE JARGON SWEEP CAUGHT MY OWN COPY on its first run. The first draft said "this
window is still holding, and a window does not empty itself"; "window" is the
engine's word, not an operator's. The copy was reworded and the assertion was
NOT relaxed, and a further test asserts the rewording did not cost the
explanation its content -- a jargon fix that deletes the point is not a fix. The
sweep is scoped to the block this slice added, because the panel as a whole says
"records in snapshot", which is settled vocabulary a previous slice decided on,
and a test that fights settled vocabulary gets deleted.

The explanation appears ONLY where it is true: not at a non-zero rate, not at zero
records, not with no measurement yet, and not when a sub-second rate rounds down
to zero on screen -- claiming nothing arrived when something did is worse than
showing nothing.

SLICE 6 -- TWO SURFACES THAT DIFFER ON SCREEN.

.live HAD NO RULE IN styles.css AT ALL. The live panel rendered with whatever its
container gave it, while the record panel arrived with its own ground, border and
header. So the two-truths contract was TRUE IN MARKUP AND FALSE ON SCREEN: a
reader had to tell the two panels apart by their contents, which is the opposite
of structural separation. Every JSX assertion in this repository passed while one
of the two surfaces had no style, which is the strongest argument for a guard
that reads the stylesheet as well as the components.

Both surfaces are now given grounds and edges that differ, so the distinction
survives greyscale and a printed page. Each carries data-surface, and a guard
asserts they never nest, that each names itself in words, that no recorded figure
lacks an age, and -- the anti-swing -- that nothing in the record surface
animates, because an animation on a recorded value claims it is happening now.

The dead CSS went with the dead control. .selector, .selector__label,
.selector__input and .app__select are removed rather than left behind: orphaned
CSS is the same trap as orphaned code, unreachable and passing every assertion,
and the next person to add a control of that name would have inherited a layout
nobody chose.

Project Law 1 (a test that pins jargon defends jargon) is applied to the new copy
above, not just to the existing view. Project Law 3 is respected by asserting on
rendered text wherever the claim is about reading; the one text read of the
stylesheet follows the precedent css-collision-guard.test.ts set, because the
rule being defended is a rule about declarations, which is what text is.
